# Q-P2-001 (from coding planner P2, round 5) → R-JOINT

**Concerns:** EXP-1 §2.2 "Revised version" (W = one dihedral gradient per rotatable bond on TD's quadruple rule
`torsional-diffusion/utils/standardization.py:69-90`), §5.1 `flexitors/geometry/jacobian.py`; E-JOINT-032; D-004.

**Question.**
1. The TD rule takes `n0[0]` and `n1[0]` (`standardization.py:84-87`), i.e. the first neighbour in graph order, which
   can be a hydrogen. When a quadruple is H-terminated, P_W fixes the H-dihedral about the bond, but the heavy-atom
   dihedral about the same bond can still change through "L" (the H–X–heavy improper/angle terms). The geometry head
   could then do heavy-atom torsion work that is booked as L. Should W use (a) the TD rule exactly, (b) a heavy-first
   rule (lowest-index heavy neighbour, else H), or (c) all heavy-atom quadruples about each rotatable bond (which
   over-constrains angles at sp3 centres)?
2. TD treats triple bonds as rotatable (123 in the QM9 test set, E-CHEM-050/060), and quadruples next to a linear
   segment have a bond angle near 180°, where the dihedral gradient scales as 1/sin θ and the dihedral is undefined.
   Should such rows be dropped from W (and counted), and at what angle threshold?

**Decision at stake:** the definition of W, hence of the L subspace, the drift metric (EXP-1 §3 row 9) and the
"dihedral-preserving" claim.

**Default used (PENDING Q-P2-001):** (a) the TD rule exactly as the spec says; rows with min(sin θ_abc, sin θ_bcd) <
sin 10° dropped and counted; and a diagnostic of the change of **all** heavy-atom dihedrals about TD-rotatable bonds
per step and per trajectory, plus the share of TD quadruples that contain an H. (b) is available as an ablation flag.


## Answer (JOINT)
EXP-1 revised (§10.1.2): option **(b), heavy-first**.
1. **Heavy-first.** For each rotatable bond take the lowest-index heavy neighbour on each side; use H only if that
   side has no heavy neighbour.
   - Reason: with TD's first-neighbour rule (`standardization.py:84-87`), an H-terminated row lets a geometry step
     change the heavy-atom dihedral through "L". That is real torsion work booked as L, and the evaluation is
     heavy-atom RMSD.
   - The choice of quadruple does not affect TD's torsion updates, which are whole-side rotations [E-JOINT-026].
   - Option (c) over-constrains sp³ angles, as you say.
   - Keep your diagnostics: the change of all heavy dihedrals per step and per trajectory, and the H-terminated share.
2. **Drop near-linear rows** with min(sin θ_abc, sin θ_bcd) < sin 10°, and count them. If the heavy choice is the
   near-linear one, try the next neighbour first. Normalise the loss by rank(P_W) (Q-P1-001.6).
   Dihedrals are computed with exact atan2, not TD's clamped acos form (`utils/torsion.py:117-123`).
