We hypothesize that computing each protonation/tautomer microstate's free energy as a Boltzmann average over a state-specific conformer ensemble (generated by a torsional-diffusion conformer generator and scored with a machine-learned potential plus implicit solvent) reduces macro-pKa and tautomer-ratio error relative to 2D or single-conformer predictors (Uni-pKa, Epik 7, AcepKa) on conformationally sensitive drug-like molecules (intramolecular-H-bond-capable, zwitterionic, multi-site ionisable), because the relative stability of these microstates depends on state-specific low-energy conformations that topology-only models cannot represent.
