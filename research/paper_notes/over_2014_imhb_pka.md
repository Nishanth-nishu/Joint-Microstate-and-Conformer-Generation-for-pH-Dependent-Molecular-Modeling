# Over et al. 2014 — stereospecific IMHB shifts an experimental pKa by ~1 unit

## Citation
Over B. et al. "Impact of Stereospecific Intramolecular Hydrogen Bonding on Cell Permeability and
Physicochemical Properties." J. Med. Chem. 57(6):2746–2754 (2014). DOI 10.1021/jm500059t
(PMC3968888). Checked 2026-10-26.

## Fulltext status
full_text_read (PMC).

## Finding (the magnitude question)
Eight stereoisomers of one T. cruzi inhibitor — identical 2D constitution apart from
stereochemistry. Tertiary-amine pKa (Table 2): trans-C8,C9 (IMHB-forming) 6.08–6.18;
cis-C8,C9 (no IMHB) 7.07–7.16 — "a pKa difference of ∼1 unit for the tertiary amine" (Results).
Measured by pH-metric/spectrophotometric titration (Sirius GLpKa, 25 °C, argon, n=3).
IMHB proven by 1H–15N HMBC ("definitively proves the existence of a hydrogen bond", Results),
ROESY and solvent-dependence of N28-H; QM thermodynamic cycle (B3LYP/6-31G*, SM8/PB on MMFFs
conformers) reproduced ΔpKa = 1.1.

## Relevance to p(z,x|G,h)
Direct experimental evidence that the *conformational preference* (an intramolecular H-bond
available only in one diastereomer) shifts a protonation equilibrium by ~1 pKa unit ≈ 1.4 kcal/mol
≈ an order of magnitude in state population at a given pH. Caveat: here conformation is enforced by
stereochemistry, so it is a different-molecule comparison, not a within-molecule conformer-coupling
measurement. Honest reading: it bounds the *size* of conformation-linked pKa effects at ~1 unit for
drug-like amines with IMHBs; it does not by itself prove that a flexible molecule's microstate
populations must be modelled conformer-by-conformer.
